# Imported lean beef fell in each of the four months to September, to 300.6 US cents/lb, 13.2% below May

Month to September 2026. Prepared 19 November 2026 by Jeffy Joseph Johny.

## What changed
- The IMF price for Australian and New Zealand frozen lean beef was 300.6 US cents/lb in September: down 4.3% on August and 3.0% on September 2025. The 12-month high was 351.4 in April 2026.
- In Australian dollars it was A$9.31/kg, at 0.7115 US$ per A$.
- US retail ground beef has not followed: US$6.79/lb in September, still 7.4% above a year earlier.

## Possible drivers (strongest evidence first)
- **More Australian beef sent to the US (likely).** China added a 55% tariff on Australian beef from 20 June, after Australia filled its 205,000 t quota. Australian shipments to the US hit 49,207 t in June, up 39%. *(Sources rows 6 and 8)*
- **Higher US imports overall (likely).** US beef imports were 542 million lb in July, up 19%, led by Argentina under its extra 80,000 t quota. *(Rows 10 and 11)*
- **A bigger low-tariff quota for lean trimmings from 1 September (likely, September only).** 300,000 t over three months, mainly open to Brazil. *(Row 13)*
- **Comfortable stocks (possible).** US beef in cold storage was 407 million lb on 31 August, up 5%. *(Row 12)*
- **Softer retail demand (possible).** The CPI beef index fell 1.0% in September. *(Row 20)*

## What remains uncertain
- US cow slaughter, the main domestic source of lean beef, was 5.5% lower than a year earlier in July. That supports prices and works against the fall. *(Row 16)*
- My data cannot separate how much each driver contributed.
- Model check: over the last 12 months, AutoML missed by 12.9 US cents/lb on average 1 to 3 months ahead, against 13.7 for the naive forecast. Neither caught the August and September fall. AutoML's 80% range for October to December is 266.6 to 323.9, so treat the outlook as a range, not a point.
